# Promo Cards

Promo cards are match angles earned from card packs. Most are one-off stories that give a single match a hook and a rarity-based hype boost without requiring later chapters. **Best Frenemies** is an exception: after the match, it forms the featured wrestler and their selected opponent into an active tag team.

## Acquiring Cards

New campaigns start with five distinct Match types (one Legendary, one Rare, three Common), five Promo cards, and separate championship unlocks. One Promo is always **Create Your Own** and is marked received in the regular Common cycle. The other four are drawn uniformly without replacement from the six-card starter-only pool below. Their rarities are not guaranteed to form a particular mix.

| Starter Promo | Rarity | Premise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your Seat Is Taken | Common | A veteran's old privileges do not follow them into the new locker room. |
| The Wrong Channel | Rare | A rival-company loyalist is exposed by their rejected job application. |
| Marking New Territory | Legendary | A defector's logo stunt and pledge of allegiance divide two former employees. |
| The Replacement Was Already Here | Common | Two signings discover one was hired elsewhere to replace the other. |
| Who's in Charge? | Rare | A wealthy wrestler claims to have financed opening night and bought the main event. |
| All Eyez On Me | Legendary | A Death Row Records appearance and 2Pac endorsement bring a Thug Life chain and a new rival. |

Starter Promos are obtainable only in the opening pack, but owned copies remain playable afterward. Paid, tutorial, and annual packs draw exclusively from the regular Promo pool, including Create Your Own. Random-rarity draws in those packs use the standard odds: 62% Common, 29% Rare, and 9% Legendary. These changes apply to new campaigns and do not reissue cards in existing saves. References to 2Pac in the launch card are fictional alternate-history game scenarios.

The Lounge vending machine offers five differently colored packages. Paid purchases have no monthly limit; each pack costs money from the promotion's bankroll:

| Pack | Contents | Color | Price |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Match | 3 match cards | Red | $140,000 |
| Promo | 3 Promo cards | Green | $140,000 |
| Free Agent | 1 wrestler, 1 match card, 1 Promo card | Blue | $520,000 |
| Create Your Own Wrestler | 1 custom wrestler, 1 Match card, 1 Promo card | Pink | $520,000 |
| Variety | 2 match cards, 2 Promo cards | Gold | $200,000 |

Create Your Own Wrestler opens an identity form before purchase. The wrestler starts at popularity 45 with all six stats at 50; name, nickname, style, alignment, age, hometown, finisher, and biography are editable. Custom wrestlers use an initials portrait, persist with saves, and follow the normal full-roster contract queue. Invalid submissions, insufficient funds, and an unfinished pack reveal do not consume money or create a wrestler.

Each regular Promo draw rolls rarity first, then picks a card not yet received in that rarity's current cycle. Only after every regular card at that rarity has been received does its cycle reset. Exhausting Commons never upgrades a Common roll to Rare or Legendary. Cycles persist across purchases, consumption, year changes, and save/reload. Match cards in later packs can still repeat.

Old saves seed their cycles from available inventory, draw logs, and played Promo history; cards no longer present in those records cannot be recovered. Starter deck changes apply to new campaigns and do not reissue cards on reload.

## Tutorial Packs

After the third completed flagship show, the end of the broadcast automatically opens the tutorial email in the computer. Its **Visit Vending Machine** button opens the Lounge machine, where a free Free Agent Pack (one wrestler contract, one Match card, and one Promo) is waiting. This replaces the former event-three Variety Pack and event-six Free Agent reward; there is no separate event-six tutorial pack.

The contract signs immediately when there is room; otherwise it enters the saved intake queue for a retirement or pass decision. Queued wrestlers cannot be drawn again while they are waiting. Existing claims for the Free Agent tutorial remain claimed, preventing a duplicate award when loading an older save.

Each January after a completed campaign year (after flagship shows 12, 24, 36, and so on), two free Free Agent Packs and one free Variety Pack become available in the Lounge. Together they award two wrestler contracts, four Match cards, and four regular Promos. A year-end email includes a Visit Vending Machine button. These packs replace the January chip-based free-agency board and its annual Match/Promo drop; annual aging and career awards are unchanged.

Each year-end pack has its own saved claim, so unclaimed rewards carry forward and can be opened one at a time. Existing saves can claim rewards for completed years unless the old annual drop was already awarded for that year. An already-awarded annual pack reveal remains available to finish; replacement rewards do not duplicate it.

Free packs stay waiting until claimed, cost no money, and can only be claimed once. Claims and card-reveal progress survive save/reload. Both paid and free packs use the card-flip reveal screen and return to the machine without reloading the game. A waiting reveal must be finished before opening another pack. Existing campaigns past event three can claim the Free Agent tutorial reward if it has not already been claimed.

| Rarity | Match hype | Momentum | Popularity |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Common | +4 to +8 | +1 | +1 to +2 |
| Rare | +9 to +16 | +1 to +2 | +2 to +4 |
| Legendary | +18 to +30 | +2 to +3 | +4 to +7 |

The specific rewards are rolled within the rarity's ranges when the Promo is played. Card eligibility can depend on the selected wrestlers, active roster context, or current championship holders.

## Booking a Promo

In the monthly Story Promo step, choose an owned card that is eligible for the selected match pairing. A card assigned to another match is reserved and cannot be selected twice. Removing a selection returns it to the available inventory; skipping the step spends nothing.

**Coming for that #1 Spot** is a Legendary Promo. If its match has a winner, that winner becomes the promotion's #1 contender, directly behind the champions in the computer's rankings. A draw does not change the rankings.

**Best Frenemies** registers the featured wrestler and selected opponent as a duo after their match, or boosts an existing duo to maximum chemistry. This gives them the team-cohesion bonus in future tag matches. Other partner combinations are retained, not dissolved.

## Tag Duos

Booking a complete two-person side in a tag match automatically saves that duo. No manual formation is required. Wrestlers may appear in multiple duos, and the computer's Teams screen offers inline renaming. Rebooking the same pair preserves its name, chemistry, and shared win/loss/draw record. Two-person teams in other tag stipulations are recognized too; three-person sides are not recorded as duos.

The saved duo collection is capped at 20, retaining the most recently used pairs. Older duplicate or inactive entries are removed from this collection only. Championship history, show archives, and top-match records are never pruned by this limit. If a forgotten pair teams together again, it is detected and saved again.

The card is consumed when the monthly Promo is played, not when it is selected. On play, its match-hype effect is applied to that match and its wrestler rewards go to the selected spotlight wrestler. Apart from Best Frenemies' future team-cohesion effect, Promo effects do not change the booked match's expected wrestling quality or act as ongoing heat for stakes and stipulations.

**Create Your Own** is a consumable Common Promo card, drawn as part of the Common cycle. It appears alongside the other owned cards. Clicking it opens title and description fields; saving the text reserves the card without spending it. Playing it consumes one copy and rolls the same rewards as a Rare Promo: +9 to +16 hype, +1 to +2 momentum, and +2 to +4 popularity. It is not an unlimited fallback when Promo inventory is empty.

## Card Book

The open binder on the Lounge coffee table opens the card inventory. Its Wrestlers, Match, and Promo sections reuse the pack-reveal card style. Owned wrestler cards open their profiles and return to the book. Available, unsigned free agents appear greyed out; unavailable wrestlers appear as anonymous black silhouettes without portraits.

Match and Promo cards with no current stock appear greyed out. Their top-right badges separate copies held, Match cards booked for the upcoming show, and previously used copies (for example, `2x used`). Reserving a Match card is not a completed use: Match usage increments when the show runs, while Promo usage increments when the monthly Promo is played. Counts are retained across stock changes and save/reload.

Older saves recover usage from retained show archives and Promo history. Uses whose records were already discarded before this feature cannot be reconstructed.

## Profile History

Played Promos and story-related match incidents appear on the participants' wrestler profiles under **Storylines**. Each entry records the Promo, spotlight wrestler, participants, show, result, and effects. This is an archive of completed one-off moments, not an active journal or progression track.

Saves created before Promo cards are migrated on load: archived and active storyline entries become legacy profile-history entries, and their old active state and match links are discarded. The migration avoids adding a duplicate when the same legacy entry has already been converted.
